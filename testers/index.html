Closed Testing — Android

Help test it before it launches.

AI Security Management Prep is days away from Google Play. Before a new developer account can publish, Google requires a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 continuous days. That's what this page is for.

You need an Android phone and a Google account

There is no iPhone version and no web version to test. If you're on iOS, there's genuinely nothing to install — please don't spend time on the steps below. The best thing you can do is pass this page to someone on Android.

What You'd Be Testing

AI Security Management Prep — an offline study app for the AAISM™ exam.

A focused Android study companion — no account, no ads, no syncing. Everything runs on the device.

01

307 Practice Questions

Every question comes with a written explanation of why the answer is what it is — not just a right/wrong marker.

02

90-Question Practice Exam

A full simulated sitting you can run timed or untimed, depending on whether you're drilling or rehearsing.

03

Study Guide & Glossary

A condensed study guide covering all three domains, plus a 157-term glossary for looking things up quickly.

04

Fully Offline

No account, no analytics, nothing leaves the device. Works on a plane, on a subway, anywhere.

Testers get the full version free

The app will sell for $24.99. As a tester you unlock everything at no cost through Google Play's test-purchase system — you'll go through what looks like a normal checkout, but it runs on a test card rather than your own payment method. Read the warning below before you confirm anything.

How To Join

Two steps, in order.

You won't get an install link on this page — it only works once you've been approved, so it would just break if you tried it early.

01

Request to join the tester group

Go to the AI Sec Prep Testers group and ask to join. You must be signed into your Google account to see the join button — if it isn't there, that's almost always why. Use the same Google account you use on your Android phone.

02

Wait for the email with your install link

Once your request is approved, you'll get an email containing the Google Play install link. The link only works for approved group members, which is why it isn't published here. If nothing arrives within a day or so, email testing@briefcasego.com.

Read Before You Unlock

Check the payment method every time.

Before confirming any purchase, tap the payment method row

When you unlock the full version, Google Play shows a normal-looking checkout. Tap the payment method row and confirm it reads "Test card, always approves" and "This is a test order, you will not be charged."

If you see your own credit card or any real payment method instead, back out — do not confirm — and email testing@briefcasego.com. Confirming with a real card would charge you $24.99 for real, and that's a refund hassle neither of us wants.

The Commitment

14 continuous days, opted in.

This is the part that actually matters, and it's worth being blunt about. Google's rule is that at least 12 testers stay opted in for 14 continuous days. The clock doesn't start until 12 people are opted in, and it runs continuously rather than cumulatively — so if the count slips below 12 partway through, the two weeks can start over for everybody.

That makes the ask less about usage than about simply staying put: join, install it, and stay opted in for the full two weeks. Use it as much or as little as you genuinely would — light use is fine and honest feedback on light use is still useful. Once the 14 days are done you keep the app and keep the unlock; nothing gets taken back.

If you need to drop out

That's completely fine, and no hard feelings — just email testing@briefcasego.com before you opt out, so a replacement can be lined up before the count dips below 12.

What Would Help Most

Tell me where it's wrong.

Praise is nice. Problems are useful. Anything that's wrong, confusing or annoying is worth reporting, however small it feels.

Questions That Read Badly

A question that's ambiguous, awkwardly worded, or has an answer you'd argue with. Say which one and what bothered you.

Explanations That Don't Land

If an explanation doesn't actually explain — or leaves you more confused than the question did — that's exactly what needs fixing.

Anything Hard To Read Or Use

Text that's too small, contrast that's rough in bright light, a tap target that's fiddly, a screen that feels cramped on your device.

Anything Broken Or Odd

Crashes, a lost progress bar, a button that does nothing, something that behaves differently after you close and reopen the app.

Where to send it

Email testing@briefcasego.com — for feedback, for problems with joining, for payment-screen worries, or to drop out. A one-line note is fine; there's no form to fill in.
